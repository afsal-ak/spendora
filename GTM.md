# GTM.md

## Target User

The primary target user is an engineering manager or technical founder at a startup with roughly 5–50 employees that already relies heavily on AI tooling across development, writing, research, or support workflows.

These teams usually move fast, adopt multiple AI subscriptions early, and rarely revisit whether their tooling choices still make financial sense after the team grows.

The ideal user is not someone researching “AI tools.” It is someone already paying for several of them and starting to feel the operational cost.

---

## What They Search Before Wanting This Tool

The user usually notices the problem indirectly.

Typical triggers:
- unexpectedly high AI invoices
- duplicate subscriptions across teammates
- finance teams asking about software costs
- comparing Cursor vs Copilot vs Claude costs
- rising API bills

Searches are more likely to be:
- “Cursor vs Copilot pricing for teams”
- “best AI coding tool for startups”
- “reduce OpenAI API costs”
- “Claude Team pricing”
- “AI spend per engineer”

## First 100 Users Without Paid Ads

The first users would likely come from highly targeted distribution rather than broad marketing.

The plan:
- post real AI cost comparisons on Twitter/X
- create short “we audited our AI stack” style posts
- share screenshots of real audit outputs
- launch on Product Hunt
- post practical findings in founder and AI subreddits
- reach out directly to startup founders already discussing AI tooling costs

The tool itself is designed to be shareable through public audit links, which helps organic distribution.

## What Early Traction Looks Like

Week-one traction would probably look like:
- 300–500 landing page visitors
- 80–120 completed audits
- 10–20 saved reports
- 5–10 consultation requests

The strongest signal would not be raw traffic. It would be users sharing audit results publicly or forwarding them internally to teammates.
